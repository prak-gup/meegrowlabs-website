Guided Walkthrough · ~12 minutes · for CS Dte

SUVIDHA SAINIK — a script for the room

Seven chapters, one per demonstrator page, in presenter order. Every step names what to click, what the audience sees, and the point it proves against PS-11. The OTP used throughout is 246810.

  1. Chapter 1 of 7 · ~2 min

    The beneficiary

    PS-11 AIM #1 — visibility of own PI data. Open on beneficiary.html, the INDL mobile app.

    1

    Log in — card + OTP, then give DPDP consent

    What to click

    On beneficiary.html, log in with a card number, enter OTP 246810, then accept the DPDP consent screen.

    What they see

    A two-factor login (card + mobile OTP) and an explicit consent step naming what data is used and why, before any PI data is shown.

    The point it proves

    Login gates everything, and consent is asked before PI data is shown — not assumed.

    2

    My Card → Entitlement balance → Nearest URC

    What to click

    Open My Card (barcode, Grocery/Liquor Card ID, validity), then Entitlement (Grocery, Liquor, AFD I/II — overall vs. balance), then Nearest URC.

    What they see

    The exact PS-11 field list rendered as-is: card IDs, validity, pay level, status; overall/balance meters; the nearest URC's code, address, timings and weekly off.

    The point it proves

    This is the sponsor's own field list, delivered — not a reinterpretation of it.

    3

    Lodge a complaint and watch it route itself

    What to click

    Open Contact Us, type a complaint, submit.

    What they see

    SAMADHAN classifies the complaint live and routes it to the right nodal officer with an SLA timer, without a human dispatcher in the loop.

    The point it proves

    Grievance intake is instant and self-routing — the 45-day manual chain (Unit CO → Area Depot → CS Dte) is not the only path.

    4

    Switch persona — War Widow, then ESM

    What to click

    Use the persona switcher on beneficiary.html to log in as a War Widow (no liquor card), then as an Ex-Serviceman.

    What they see

    The same screens, but entitlement categories and card fields change to match the beneficiary's actual status — a War Widow has no liquor entitlement; an ESM's validity is tied to service record, not a serving unit.

    The point it proves

    This works across CSD's whole beneficiary spectrum, not just serving personnel.

  2. Chapter 2 of 7 · ~3 min

    The counter — PRAMANIK

    Pillar II, the hero of this demonstrator. Verifies the person, not just the card. Open on urc.html.

    1

    Ring up a liquor sale, ₹5,600, at URC Delhi Cantt (Tier 1)

    What to click

    On urc.html, select URC Delhi Cantt, start a liquor sale for ₹5,600 against a demo beneficiary's card.

    What they see

    Because the amount exceeds ₹5,000 at a Tier 1 URC, all three PRAMANIK factors activate: F1 card/QR scan, F2 mobile OTP (enter 246810), F3 Aadhaar yes/no.

    The point it proves

    The card is authenticated, but so is the person presenting it — three factors for the highest-value, highest-misuse transaction category.

    2

    BADE clears, sale completes

    What to click

    Continue through the BADE anomaly check, then Complete Sale.

    What they see

    A pass/fail anomaly check with its evidence shown (no spike, no cross-URC conflict), then a completed, logged transaction.

    The point it proves

    A clean sale is fast — PRAMANIK does not slow down the 99% of legitimate transactions.

    3

    Try a deceased-record card — HELD, not blocked

    What to click

    Start a sale against the card flagged as a deceased record in the demo data.

    What they see

    BADE flags the transaction and holds it for the URC Manager's decision — it does not refuse the sale outright.

    The point it proves

    Flag, don't block — a false positive never turns into a genuine beneficiary being humiliated at the counter.

    4

    Manager decides, with remarks — logged to audit

    What to click

    From the held queue, open the item, enter remarks, and release or decline it.

    What they see

    A named JCO's decision, with remarks, immediately visible on audit.html.

    The point it proves

    The AI advises, a named officer decides — and that decision is permanent record, not a silent override.

    5

    Switch to a Tier 3 forward URC — offline-first

    What to click

    Select a Tier 3 URC (e.g. a Kargil or Poonch forward URC) and start a sale.

    What they see

    Only F1 (card scan) runs immediately; F2 OTP is deferred and queued for end-of-day sync when connectivity permits.

    The point it proves

    PRAMANIK degrades gracefully at the front line — it never makes connectivity a precondition for a soldier getting his ration.

  3. Chapter 3 of 7 · ~1.5 min

    The URC Manager

    PS-11 AIM #2 — visibility of URC data to the URC Manager. Still on urc.html.

    1

    My URC, My QD, and Manager details

    What to click

    Open the My URC tab, then Manager Details.

    What they see

    URC code, name, address, PIN, contact, weekly off, My QD — and the manager's own rank, name, tenure and contact, exactly per the sponsor's field list.

    The point it proves

    The URC Manager sees their own unit's operating data, not a subset filtered by a third party.

    2

    Bronze & Steel cards, and the held queue

    What to click

    Open Bronze Cards and Steel Cards, then the Held Queue.

    What they see

    The Bronze/Steel card registers (Card ID, Number, Validity) and every anomaly currently awaiting this manager's decision.

    The point it proves

    Everything a JCO needs to run the counter is in one screen — no separate ledger, no phone call to the depot.

  4. Chapter 4 of 7 · ~1.5 min

    Grievances — SAMADHAN

    Pillar III. Open on samadhan.html.

    1

    Paste a Hinglish complaint, watch it classify live

    What to click

    Paste a mixed Hindi-English complaint into the intake box.

    What they see

    The classifier highlights the matched terms as it types, assigns a category (entitlement, card, URC conduct, quality, pricing) and a nodal officer, live.

    The point it proves

    The classification is explainable — the matched terms are visible, not a black-box label.

    2

    Open an SLA-breached complaint, apply a precedent, resolve

    What to click

    Open a complaint marked SLA-breached (card issue), review the matched precedent, apply it, and mark resolved.

    What they see

    A similar, already-resolved complaint suggested as a template, and the SMS status notification the beneficiary receives.

    The point it proves

    45–60 days shrinks to minutes for the routine cases, with the beneficiary kept informed at every stage, not left waiting.

  5. Chapter 5 of 7 · ~1.5 min

    Supply — MLDFE & DRISHTIKON

    Pillars I & IV. Open on supply.html.

    1

    Leh thermal-innerwear forecast → approve redistribution

    What to click

    Open the demand forecast for Leh Stn URC (thermal innerwear), then Approve Redistribution from Delhi Depot.

    What they see

    A seasonal-spike forecast for a high-altitude URC, and a one-click, AI-recommended inter-depot transfer that a depot officer approves by name.

    The point it proves

    Redistribution becomes proactive, not reactive — surplus at one depot meets shortage at another before either becomes a complaint.

    2

    A red-flagged vendor, linked to insect complaints

    What to click

    Open the vendor compliance scoreboard, find the vendor scored Red, and open the linked grievances.

    What they see

    The compliance score traced directly to specific quality complaints (insects in packaged goods) filed through SAMADHAN — the two systems share evidence.

    The point it proves

    A vendor's score isn't a guess — it's built from the beneficiary's own complaints.

    3

    A GeM price benchmarking flag

    What to click

    Open the price benchmarking panel and find a SKU flagged above GeM rate.

    What they see

    CSD's procurement price sitting alongside the GeM rate and open-market MRP, with the deviation called out for the procurement committee.

    The point it proves

    Procurement anomalies surface automatically, instead of waiting for the next CAG audit.

  6. Chapter 6 of 7 · ~1 min

    Command — DRISHTI

    Pillar V. Open on drishti.html.

    1

    Heatmap, fraud monitor, weekly brief — acknowledge item 1

    What to click

    Scan the schematic national heatmap, open the fraud risk monitor, then the weekly AI brief, and Acknowledge the top item as Command.

    What they see

    URC service status and complaint density across depots; PRAMANIK/BADE-flagged transactions ranked by severity; a five-item weekly brief in plain language, with an acknowledgement captured against a named officer.

    The point it proves

    CS Dte gets one situational-awareness picture, not 4,000 separate URC ledgers — and even at this level, a decision is named and recorded, not left to the dashboard.

  7. Chapter 7 of 7 · ~1 min

    The audit trail

    Everything above, named. Open on audit.html.

    1

    Find every decision made in the last six chapters

    What to click

    Open audit.html and locate the held-sale decision, the grievance resolution, the redistribution approval, and the command acknowledgement — then export the CSV.

    What they see

    A single, chronological, immutable log — who decided, what they decided, when, and on what evidence.

    The point it proves

    Nothing in this demonstrator is opaque. Every AI-assisted step ends in a named human decision, and that decision is exportable, auditable record.

Questions this demo cannot answer — for CS Dte
  • The exact entitlement rules per beneficiary category and pay level.
  • The definition of "My QD" and of Bronze vs. Steel cards, as CS Dte intends them.
  • Which systems of record are authoritative for card validity and deceased status — SPARSH, PCDA, or unit record offices.
  • AFD and Renewal Portal API availability, and the terms of access to them.
  • The URC POS landscape — how many of the ~4,000 URCs have digitised billing today.
  • Connectivity realistically available at each URC, by tier.
  • What data-sharing approvals are needed across SPARSH, PCDA, AFD and UIDAI before any of this can touch real data.

Proposed next step: a 30-URC Proof of Concept across plains, hill and high-altitude depots (concept paper §14) — sized to answer these questions with evidence, before any wider rollout.
